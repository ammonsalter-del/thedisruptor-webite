How to Play The Disruptor

The Disruptor puts you in the cockpit of Chief Innovation Officer at Vélox Dynamics, a €420M Belgian fitness-equipment manufacturer, and challenges you to navigate the tension between heritage engineering and digital disruption over 3 simulated years. The game flows continuously, events arrive dynamically, and a connected set of managerial practices and systems competes for your attention and resources.

Quick Start

Play Online

Press Play and the game opens instantly in your browser. No installation required.

Keep a Copy Offline

The game is an HTML file with its pictures and a few companion pages, among them the board’s meetings and the mini-games. Keep a copy of the whole folder and the simulation plays without a connection, on any modern browser. Handy for classrooms with patchy wifi. Offline, the opening video and the case study do not load.

Read the Case Study

The Vélox Dynamics case study (20 to 30 min read) prepares you for the simulation. Students who read the case treat it as a strategic simulation, not a resource puzzle.

Built-In Tutorials

Two tutorial modes are built in. A Quick Guide covers eight short topics (among them your dashboard, the R&D pipeline, strategy, and tips for success) and gets you playing within minutes. A Full Guide walks through every managerial practice and system in depth, with explanations of every panel, every decision type, and every metric. Experienced gamers skip straight to playing; cautious players read everything first. Both approaches work.

System Requirements

Browser & Device

  • Modern browser: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge (latest versions)
  • Desktop or laptop recommended: mobile works but the experience is optimised for larger screens
  • Landscape orientation on tablets for best results

How Time Works

The game flows continuously: time advances week by week, with events arriving, projects progressing, and competitors moving in real time. You can pause, speed up, or slow down the simulation at any point.

Continuous Flow, Not Turns

Unlike turn-based games, The Disruptor runs as a continuous simulation. Weeks tick by, events fire, staff work on projects, and the market evolves while you make decisions across the firm's managerial systems. You can intervene at any time (hire staff, launch projects, form alliances, respond to events) without waiting for a "next turn" button. Each quarter the R&D Director sends you a brief, and each year turns on the board’s meetings. The clock stops by itself while a decision window is open, or when unanswered decisions pile up; otherwise it keeps running.

Speed Controls

Pause the simulation to think, or accelerate through quiet periods. Three speed settings let you control the pace.

Events & News

Weekly events arrive dynamically: market shifts, competitor moves, staff issues, technology breakthroughs. You respond as they happen.

The Board’s Year

The management board meets at the start of each year to settle the firm’s direction, and questions you at the end of the first and second years. These are the key milestones. Everything else flows continuously.

The Managerial Practices & Systems

A set of interconnected managerial practices and systems competes for your budget and staff. See the About page for the full list. The core tension: every euro and every person you commit to one system is unavailable for others.

R&D Portfolio → Launchpad

Build a portfolio of projects across three horizons, starting from the handful you inherit. When projects complete, prepare innovations through readiness activities, then launch to market. Timing and sequencing matter.

People & Culture

Hire across ten disciplines, set salaries, shape culture through more than twenty organisational policies, and decide on the AI & Innovation tab how far AI goes in R&D. Staff capacity is the hardest constraint: every system draws from the same pool.

External Innovation

Alliances (230+ partners), university partnerships, corporate venturing (more than forty start-ups), technology scouting, and crowdsourcing. Each extends your reach, but carries risk and cost.

Platform, IP & Standards

Grow Vélox+, manage patents and trade secrets, engage with 38 industry standards. Platform amplifies launches. IP protects revenue. Standards gate market access.

The Board’s Year

Each year has the same shape. It opens with the management board meeting on the firm’s direction: members argue for where Vélox should go, and the CEO, Katrien De Wolf, decides whether to stay the course, shift the emphasis or change strategy. A pack on your desk then sets out the year’s goal for innovation income and the objectives that go with it.

The End-of-Year Appraisal

At the end of the first and second years you go before the board. Its members question you about the year (what you funded, what you launched, what you left undone) and you answer. Katrien closes the meeting with a score: exceeds expectations, meets expectations, or needs development.

Behind the meeting the board weighs your innovation income against the year’s goal and the objectives you met. The outcome moves the next year’s budget up or down, and a poor enough record can end your appointment early. There is no appraisal after the third year: the tenure ends instead, and you are judged on the whole of it.

On Probation, the first year’s appraisal leads to a Probation Review: the board gives its view of your year, and you choose whether to carry on into the full term or end there.

Headline Income Targets

Your targets for innovation income are cumulative and rise each year. These are the Forge's; the other modes and the Incubator set their own. The board expects measurable innovation outcomes alongside commercial results.

Strategic Tips for New Players

These insights will help you navigate your first playthrough more effectively.

Read the Case Study First

The Vélox Dynamics case study prepares you for the simulation. Without it, you'll treat the game as a resource puzzle rather than a strategic simulation. 20 to 30 minutes of reading makes a massive difference.

Embrace the Tension

The game's core dynamic is the tension between heritage engineering and digital disruption. Don't ignore the Foundation's mission: it's real, it's constraint, and it creates the challenge. How you navigate this tension defines your strategy.

Understand Horizon Balance

Invest across all three horizons. Horizon 1 (incremental) delivers near-term revenue. Horizon 2 (adjacent) builds capabilities. Horizon 3 (transformational) creates future opportunities. The board expects balance.

Manage Your People

Your 67-person R&D organisation is your greatest asset and your greatest vulnerability. High morale enables ambitious projects. Low morale causes key staff to leave. Invest in culture, engagement, and career development.

Launch Carefully

Too many launches in quick succession cause revenue collapse and brand damage. Plan launches across quarters. Quality over quantity. The Innovation Launchpad takes each idea through incubation to launch.

Watch Your Competitors

Ten competitors make moves as the game runs, and the ones that threaten Vélox most move most often. Pay attention to what they're doing. Competitive intelligence is not optional: it's essential for strategic positioning.

The End of Your Tenure

When your mandate ends, or if the board ends it early, you are judged twice: by Vélox, and by the game.

What You Receive

The end screen sets out each year with the board: your innovation income against the goal, and the objectives you met. Then comes the board’s view of your tenure, and beside it The Disruptor’s score, the game’s own view, with a short word on what you did well and what to try next time. You can download your report, Your tenure at Vélox: what you chose each year, what the board said, and the game’s assessment, area by area and in words, of how you used the tools of corporate entrepreneurship and innovation management. It gives concrete evidence for reflective essays and class discussion. A postcard of your tenure comes with it.
