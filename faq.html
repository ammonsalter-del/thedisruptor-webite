Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about The Disruptor: what it is, what it costs (nothing), and how it works in teaching. The educators page has the detail.

For students

What is The Disruptor?

A free, single-player business simulation where you take the role of Chief Innovation Officer at Vélox Dynamics, a Belgian fitness equipment company. You manage R&D projects, alliances, intellectual property, a digital platform, corporate ventures, hiring, standards and more across a three-year tenure. It runs in the browser with no installation and no account.

Is it free?

Yes. No premium tier, no adverts, no registration. It is an open educational project, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with no fees of any kind. Commercial use requires a separate licence.

How long does a game take?

A full three-year run takes at least 45 minutes, and longer in most cases for new players. Probation mode covers one year and takes around 30 minutes. You can save the game and restart at any time. The Incubator teaching mode is designed to be spread across several short sessions rather than played in a single sitting.

How hard is it?

It is challenging. Sixteen systems compete for your attention and budget, events change your plans, and outcomes are uncertain. Most players will not get a top score on their first run. Probation gives a gentler start, the Forge is the standard game, and Gauntlet is deliberately punishing. You get better at reading the systems as you play. The game rewards experience and strategic thinking, not memorising a walkthrough.

What systems can I manage?

Sixteen, and they interact. The R&D Portfolio and Innovation Launchpad handle your pipeline from early research through development to market. Innovation Strategy sets the direction. Alliances and University partnerships connect you to external knowledge. Standards let you shape industry rules. The Platform manages your digital ecosystem. IP Management covers patents and trade secrets. Scouting and Crowdsourcing bring in ideas from outside. Venturing and Grant Funding provide alternative routes to innovation. Staff & Hiring builds your team, Organisation shapes how it works, and Corporate handles the relationship with the parent company. Competitors & Markets round out the picture. Each system has its own logic, and decisions in one affect the others. The game does not tell you which to prioritise. That is the job.

Do I need to master all sixteen systems?

No, you do not. The game gives you staff and managers who can handle the detail of individual systems on your behalf. You do not need to negotiate every alliance yourself, review every patent, or manage every collaboration. Your job is to ensure that the different systems are contributing to your goals and objectives, and to decide where to direct your attention when they are not. The players who do well are the ones who focus on the bigger strategic challenges rather than getting lost in the mechanics of each engagement. Trying to control everything yourself is one of the fastest ways to fall behind.

What are the game modes?

Five modes plus the Incubator. The Forge is the standard three-year game with all systems open. Pivot starts you mid-crisis, forcing a portfolio restructure. Jugaad strips the budget and makes you innovate with fewer resources. Gauntlet sets punishing targets against aggressive competitors. Probation gives you one year to prove yourself, with gentler expectations. The Incubator is a teaching variant where systems unlock progressively. Each mode creates a different strategic problem.

What is Probation?

A one-year variant of the Forge. The board gives you a single year to prove yourself, with gentler targets. It takes around 30 minutes and covers the core systems without the full three-year commitment. Probation is a good way to learn the game before taking on a full tenure, and it works well as a first classroom session.

What are the mini-games?

Three arcade-style games that appear at set points during a run. Design Thinking Pinball rewards capability boosts through a pinball mechanic. Lead User Patrol is a defender game where you spot unmet customer needs from lead users, feeding your scouting missions. Wellness Ecosystem Chase builds platform complementors and ecosystem partnerships. They are optional, but they reward you with in-game boosts and break up the management decisions with something different.

What is the Pantheon of Innovation?

A gallery of forty scholars from the field of innovation management and corporate entrepreneurship whose ideas inform the game. It includes Schumpeter, Freeman, Pavitt, Nelson, Teece, Chesbrough, von Hippel, Christensen, Penrose, Tushman, Henderson, Eisenhardt, and many others. Each scholar has a biography and key works, and some offer contextual advice during play that connects what you are doing in the simulation to their research.

What mistakes do players make?

Not spending the resources the board gives them. Being unable to kill innovations that are not working, because they have already sunk time and money into them. Being unwilling to take risk on uncertain projects, chasing safe incremental bets instead. Not using the full range of systems available: alliances, grants, scouting, standards all extend what the firm can do, but players ignore them. Chasing short-term income rather than building capabilities and taking long-term bets on new products, categories and services. These are the same patterns you see in real firms, and the debrief is where they become visible.

What happens if I get fired?

You still get the full case study PDF, the same report you would receive at the end of a completed tenure. It records what you did, what went wrong, and where the board lost confidence. Read it, then start again. A dismissal is not wasted time. You made decisions under uncertainty, they had consequences, and the report sets both out clearly enough to learn from. The simulation is a safe place to take risks that would be unthinkable in a real firm, and some of the most useful debriefs come from playthroughs that ended early.

Why is every run different?

The game draws from a large pool of events, and a single playthrough only sees a fraction of them. The board strategy vote at the start reshapes the CEO's expectations. The five modes create different strategic problems. Competitors behave differently each time. Two students can play the same mode and have quite different experiences.

Is it worth playing more than once?

There is more in the game than any single playthrough can show you. Events are drawn from a large pool, and each time the game deals a different hand, but the bigger reason to go back is that the sixteen systems interact in ways you will not see first time round. Most players spend their first go working out what the systems do. On later playthroughs they start to see the trade-offs between them, and to try strategies they would not have risked before. There is no single winning formula. The board strategy, the mode, and the events all shift the ground, so what worked once may not work again. The point is to learn, to raise your awareness of the challenges and complexity of managing innovation and corporate entrepreneurship. That is what makes the game a space for experimentation rather than a test to be passed.

What is the end-of-game scorecard?

A multi-page PDF case study of your tenure, covering performance across every dimension: portfolio balance, capability development, IP, partnerships, platform health, revenue and board approval. It is mapped to the ISO 56001 Innovation Management Standard and gives you an IMS Maturity Score (0 to 100), a Legacy Score (A to D) and a narrative of what happened. The case study provides evidence for reflective essays and class discussion.

Where should I start?

Start with Probation in the Forge to learn the systems without the full three-year commitment. If you have time, read the Vélox Dynamics case study first. It sets the context and helps you treat the game as a strategic simulation rather than a resource puzzle. Once you have found your feet, play a full three-year Forge run. The built-in tutorials cover the dashboard and every system.

Can I get help while playing?

Yes. The game has two built-in tutorial modes: a Quick Guide covering five topics to get you playing in minutes, and a Full Guide walking through every system. There is also a free Player's AI Assistant, hosted on Google's NotebookLM, that answers questions about the game in plain language. It is spoiler-free and will not reveal scoring thresholds or end-of-tenure mechanics.

Why Belgium?

Most business teaching quietly defaults to a United States setting. Placing Vélox Dynamics in Belgium puts students in a different and real institutional world: European universities, enterprise-foundation ownership, EU funding and standards. Rooting the company in a specific place and culture gives students something concrete to care about before the decisions begin.

Why the fitness equipment industry?

Because it is tangible and familiar, and it stretches across the whole innovation problem. Everyone understands fitness equipment, so no student is shut out by unfamiliar technology. Yet these firms sell to consumers and to businesses, and increasingly run a digital platform alongside the hardware, so a single company reaches from physical products and materials through connected services, data and AI to medical rehabilitation and sustainability. That breadth lets the player face the full range of innovation management decisions.

What is the Foundation, and why does it matter?

Vélox Dynamics is listed on Euronext Brussels, but the Van Marcke Foundation for Health and Movement controls it through a dual-class share structure, holding 78% of the voting rights. The Foundation has a mission around health, movement science, and wellness, and the game tracks how well your decisions as CIO align with it across three pillars: environmental, social, and governance. Your choices affect the Purpose score — forming the right alliances can strengthen it, ignoring a supply chain scandal can damage it, and the board will let you know if you are drifting. The commercial side of the business still needs revenue, launches, and market share. Managing both at once is the tension at the heart of the game.

Are you collecting my data?

No. The game runs entirely in your browser. There is no account, no sign-in and nothing to submit. No data is collected, no cookies are set, and nothing is sent to a server. Game state lives in memory during the session and is discarded when you close the tab.

Can I suggest features or report bugs?

Yes. Email ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk or use the Issues tab on the GitHub repository. Players' ideas have shaped every version of the game.

What if I see the game being misused?

The game is free for education and research, and the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence is there to keep it that way. If you come across anyone putting it behind a paywall, selling access, using it in paid consulting or corporate training without a licence, or passing off the materials as their own, please let us know at ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk. Reports help protect the project for everyone who uses it.

For educators

What do students need?

A modern web browser. The game sets no cookies, holds no student data, and runs entirely client-side. Students open a link and play.

What does it teach?

Innovation management and corporate entrepreneurship together, through running the innovation function of an established firm. Around twenty-five concepts from the research literature are built into its systems, among them absorptive capacity, organisational ambidexterity, appropriability and value capture, dynamic capabilities, open innovation, path dependence, platform strategy and disruptive innovation. Students meet the ideas in context as they play. The in-game Pantheon of Innovation introduces the relevant scholar at each decision point, connecting the simulation directly to the field. The Educator's Companion maps each system to the underlying theory.

What courses and levels does it suit?

Innovation management, corporate entrepreneurship, technology strategy, R&D management and related subjects, at undergraduate, postgraduate, MBA, executive-education and doctoral level. The game modes let you match difficulty to the cohort: Probation or Incubator for first-timers, Forge for the standard experience, Pivot or Jugaad for specialist topics, Gauntlet for a serious challenge.

Do I need to train my students to play?

No. You post a link. The game has built-in tutorials, help throughout, and the Player's AI Assistant for anyone who wants more. Students can replay as often as they like. Some play once, others go back several times chasing a better score. How much further you go is up to you: the Educator's Pack is there if you want to tie it into your course with session plans, debrief questions and assessment ideas.

Do I need to master the game to use it?

No. Many colleagues have used the game with their students without ever playing it as a game themselves. The game teaches concepts and ideas that most educators in this field understand well already. The Educator's Pack was created to lower the challenges of adoption, and the Educator's AI Assistant can answer questions before class and in real time as they come up. Some of your students will end up knowing the game far better than you. Gamers will look for hacks to gain advantage, just as entrepreneurs do. That is part of the learning.

What is the Incubator?

A teaching variant of the Forge designed to control how students encounter the game's sixteen systems. Students begin with two playable systems (Portfolio and Innovations) and unlock others progressively. Both educators and students can unlock systems, so it works whether you set the pace or let students explore on their own. The CIO cannot be dismissed, so students can experiment without the risk of an early game over. It maps directly to a syllabus: if you are teaching alliances this week, unlock alliances; next week, unlock IP strategy. Students play between sessions and bring their experience to class, and each unlock introduces a new layer of complexity. By the end of a module, students are managing all sixteen systems and can see how the decisions connect.

How do I use the Incubator in a course?

Most educators assign it as between-session homework. Each week you or the students unlock the system that matches your teaching topic, students play for 30 to 45 minutes outside class, and the class session opens with a structured debrief: what happened when you tried alliances, how did IP strategy change your approach, what trade-offs did you face. The Incubator works well across 6 to 10 weeks. Some colleagues use it differently: a concentrated two-day workshop, a flipped classroom where the simulation replaces pre-reading, or a capstone exercise where students write up their full tenure as a reflective case. The Educator's Pack includes a suggested unlock sequence matched to a typical innovation management syllabus and session plans for several formats.

Can I use it for assessment?

Through reflection rather than the score. The game scores each player at the end, but that is feedback, not a grade: outcomes carry a large element of chance, so the score alone is a poor measure of learning. What works well is a reflective essay. Every run produces a PDF report the student keeps, and they write about the decisions they made, why, and what they would do differently. The Educator's Pack includes assignment ideas built around this approach.

How does it connect to innovation management research?

The game's systems are designed around concepts from the innovation management and corporate entrepreneurship research literature. The in-game Pantheon of Innovation is a gallery of forty scholars from the field, including Freeman, Schumpeter, Pavitt, Nelson, Teece, Chesbrough, von Hippel, Christensen, Penrose, Tushman, Cohen, Levinthal, Henderson, Eisenhardt, and many others. Each has a biography, key works, and contextual advice that appears during play. The Educator's Companion maps each of the sixteen systems to the relevant research, so you can connect what happened in the simulation to the concepts in your syllabus.

Is it flexible?

Very. It runs from a thirty-minute Probation session to a full-semester module, and from first-year undergraduates to executives, with five modes and the Incubator's progressive unlock. With no install and no account, it drops into a lecture, a workshop, homework or an online class without setup.

Is there an educator pack?

Yes, and it is free on request from ammon.salter@wbs.ac.uk. It includes a teaching companion, assignment bank with rubrics, exam question bank, student onboarding materials, an introduction slide deck, and two AI assistants (one for educators, one for students). The educators page lists everything in the pack.

Is the game still being developed?

Yes. The current version is 1.0.2. Since its launch on 26 April 2026, the game has been accessed by over 600 people from 50 countries, and educators from 31 universities have used it in their teaching. Development is continuous, with bug fixes, balance adjustments and new features driven by playtesting feedback. The game is open source on GitHub, and contributions are welcome.

If it is so good, why is it free?

Because it was built by an academic, not a company, and giving it away is part of the point. Credit comes through citations rather than sales. The game is free for education and research; commercial users pay for a licence, which helps keep it that way. There is no premium version behind the free one. What you play is the whole game.

How does it compare with other innovation management simulations?

Commercial simulations are typically licensed per student, need accounts and setup, and often cover a narrower range of topics. The Disruptor is free and open source, runs in the browser with no setup and no student accounts, covers sixteen interconnected systems across the full breadth of innovation management and corporate entrepreneurship, and comes with a free Educator's Pack. Because the licence is open, you can adapt the game to your course rather than fitting your course around the game.
