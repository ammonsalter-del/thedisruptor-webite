Competitive Play

How Forge players compare, and your class’s results.

How it works

  1. A student starts a Forge game and chooses Compare, with your class code, or Skip.
  2. The game plays the same either way. Results are sent at the end of each year.
  3. At the end, the student sees where each year stands. You see the class here.

Notes

  • Time played is shown for the class as a whole, never for one student.
  • If you ask students which nickname is theirs, you link results to people. What you download is yours to look after.
  • Students under 18: follow your school’s rules on online tools.

What is kept

The game records the results of each Forge year and the time played. No personal data is collected. It does not track you. All play is anonymous. The data is used to improve the game and monitor use.

Recorded: each year’s income against its goal, objectives met, the board’s appraisal and score, what was used (launches, projects, partners, grants, programmes, missions, hires), time played, how the tenure ended, and the game’s own assessment.

Compare adds a nickname made up by the game, a level and a class code. Skip adds nothing. Other versions send nothing.

Never recorded: a name, anything typed, times of day. The database keeps no IP address. Nothing is kept on the device.

For a class: its label, level and code. No name, e-mail or password.

Where and how long: a database in the EU (Supabase, under its data processing agreement). Nicknames and class codes are removed after 12 months; results are kept.
